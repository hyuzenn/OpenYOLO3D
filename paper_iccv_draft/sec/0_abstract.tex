\begin{abstract}
Streaming 3D perception must emit objects immediately, yet its consumers need one
physical object to stay one object. Temporal confirmation, which reports a track only after $N$
observations, is the standard remedy, but it either delays those observations
or discards them.
We therefore propose \emph{provisional emission with retrospective revision}:
every observation is emitted at its own frame under a provisional status, and
later evidence confirms, relabels or retracts it instead of withholding it. A four-frame retraction horizon exactly reproduces the unbounded
provisional output. On nuScenes it retains $160{,}938$ observations
($+43.6\%$) that prefix-deleting causal emission discards, at zero emission
latency, and revision raises AMOTA by $0.0823$ --- while giving up DetA, HOTA
and IDF1 against that arm. On ScanNet200 the same rule reduces class-label
switching by $26.9\%$ at a matched identity count.
At matched detection and identity budgets, and against a confidence-accumulation lifecycle, temporal
confirmation's association gain is real but is paid for in detection recall,
DetA and HOTA. The gain is bought rather than added.
A second detector narrows it further: it holds in the sensor frame and is zero
or negative in the world frame. We offer a different
operating point in the detection--association--latency--revisability
trade-off, not a uniformly better tracker.
\end{abstract}
