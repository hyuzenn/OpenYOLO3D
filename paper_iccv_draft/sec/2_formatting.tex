Throughout this paper, \emph{module} denotes a reusable post-processing
operator composed of three existing parts --- a class-agnostic associator, the
standard confirmation test for track initialization, and an emission policy
--- applied to frozen detector output (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). The module is deliberately minimal:
no part of it changes a box's coordinates, score, or label, and it decides
only \emph{which} detections enter the output stream and under \emph{which}
persistent identity. The confirmation test descends directly from
tracking-by-detection practice~\cite{sort,deepsort,ab3dmot}; the
retrospective-emission completion and the controlled evidence for what the
composition does (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}) are what we add.

\subsection{Streaming pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
Per-frame detections (a 3D box with a score and a class label) are linked
across frames into tracks by a class-agnostic nearest-centroid associator with
a $2.0$\,m bird's-eye-view center-distance threshold and a gap tolerance of
$5$ frames; no score threshold is applied before association. Association is
performed either in the \emph{sensor frame} (raw detector coordinates) or in
the \emph{world frame} (ego-motion-compensated global coordinates); both
conditions are evaluated throughout, since ego-motion compensation changes how
far the same physical object moves between frames and hence the difficulty of
association. The associator is identical in every arm of every experiment. It
is deliberately lightweight rather than state of the art, because the quantity
of interest is the difference a selection rule makes and not the highest
attainable tracking score; absolute tracking numbers reported here are
correspondingly lower than those of tuned nuScenes trackers and are not
comparable to them.

\subsection{Confirmation-based track initialization}
\label{sec:confirmation}
A newly created track is \emph{tentative}. It becomes \emph{confirmed} once it
has accumulated $N$ observations; only confirmed tracks contribute detections
to the output stream, and tracks that die before confirmation are never
emitted. We use a single operating point, $N{=}3$, throughout the paper ---
the conventional setting in tracking-by-detection systems --- rather than
tuning $N$ per dataset or per metric. The module is therefore a pure temporal
\emph{selection}: the emitted detections are a subset of the baseline
detector's output, chosen by track persistence instead of by score.

\subsection{Emission policy}
\label{sec:emission}
Confirmation leaves a policy choice for the first $N{-}1$ observations of a
track that does get confirmed (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}a). A \emph{causal}
(online) policy emits nothing until confirmation and drops the
pre-confirmation detections; an \emph{offline retrospective} policy emits the
withheld prefix at confirmation time, so a confirmed object's trajectory is
complete from its first observation.
The retrospective policy is only available to consumers that tolerate output
latency: a detection may appear up to $N{-}1$ frames after it was observed ---
at the $2$\,Hz annotated-frame rate of our outdoor benchmark, $2$ frames or
$1.0$\,s for $N{=}3$. We adopt the retrospective policy for all main results
and state this latency alongside every table; the causal policy is evaluated
as an ablation (Sec.~\ref{sec:emissionabl}), since the two policies emit
different numbers of boxes by construction.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The protocol is designed around one confound: confirmation reduces output
volume, and the detection-oriented metrics this field reports are strongly
coupled to how much a system emits, which makes a consistency gain hard to
tell apart from the effect of suppression alone. Every claim in this paper is
therefore made against a \emph{matched control} --- a non-temporal selection
rule granted the same output budget --- rather than against the unfiltered
baseline.

\subsection{Datasets and pipelines}
\label{sec:datasets}
\noindent\textbf{Outdoor.} The nuScenes~\cite{nuscenes} validation split, all
$150$ scenes at the $2$\,Hz annotated-frame rate. Detections come from a
pretrained CenterPoint~\cite{centerpoint} detector whose per-frame output is
computed once and \emph{frozen}; every arm of every experiment replays the
same stored detections ($718{,}786$ boxes over the split; mAP $0.5613$,
NDS $0.6480$), so any metric difference between arms is attributable to the
selection rule, not to detector variance.

\noindent\textbf{Indoor.} The ScanNet200~\cite{scannet200} validation split,
all $312$ scenes, in a streaming instance-segmentation pipeline: class-agnostic
3D instance-mask proposals from Mask3D~\cite{mask3d} are labeled per frame by a
2D open-vocabulary detector~\cite{yoloworld}, so each persistent instance
receives a stream of per-frame class labels. The proposal set is likewise
frozen across arms. This pipeline shares no detector, modality, or codebase
beyond the temporal module with the outdoor one, which is the point: it tests
whether the same confirmation rule transfers.

\subsection{Matched controls}
\label{sec:controls}
Two budgets are matched --- emitted detections and emitted identities
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}b) --- and under each we compare the module against a
non-temporal score rule and against the other established temporal rule.

\noindent\textbf{Detection-budget-matched control.} A score threshold on the
frozen baseline detections is calibrated so the control emits \emph{exactly} as
many boxes as the temporal module --- $257{,}259$ in the sensor frame
(threshold $0.2214$), $517{,}566$ in the world frame ($0.1272$), both with zero
count error. The match is exact because the count is a step function of the
threshold whose breakpoints are the observed scores: the matched threshold is
the $K$-th largest observed score, ties resolved upward so the count never
exceeds $K$. This control asks whether temporal selection beats the detector's
own confidence ranking at equal detection volume.

\noindent\textbf{Identity-budget-matched control.} Per sequence, the control
keeps the $K$ highest-scoring tracks (ranked by mean detection score), $K$
being the number of identities the temporal module emits there; counts match
exactly ($64{,}428$ tracks sensor, $108{,}225$ world). Five additional arms
select the same $K$ tracks \emph{at random} (five seeds), scored on association
accuracy, separating score-based selection from selection per se. The two
budgets cannot hold at once: at equal track count the control emits fewer boxes
than the module ($190{,}708$ vs.\ $360{,}309$ sensor; $608{,}168$ vs.\
$754{,}500$ world), because score-ranked tracks are shorter than
persistence-selected ones. We report this asymmetry and read the two controls
jointly.

\noindent\textbf{Confidence-accumulation control.} To compare persistence
against confidence rather than against a static score, a third control
propagates a track score by constant decay and fuses it with each matched
detection score, following CBMOT's reported best update
rule~\cite{cbmot}; its parameters are transcribed from the authors' released
implementation, not tuned here, except that the gap tolerance is our
associator's rather than theirs, since the frozen association is shared by
every arm. A single threshold on the refined score is calibrated to the same
budget as the arm it is compared with, by the rule above, so this control is
matched under both budgets. Its trained score-regression variant is not
reproduced: it is fit on nuScenes and would give the control supervision no
other arm receives.

\noindent\textbf{Indoor control.} The identity-budget design transfers
directly: the control keeps the $K$ highest-scoring instances per scene, where
$K$ matches the temporal module's emitted-instance count ($10{,}413$ instances
in total; the control realizes $10{,}410$). Three
additional arms select $K$ instances \emph{at random} per scene (three seeds),
to separate the effect of score-based selection from that of selection per se.

\noindent\textbf{Shared machinery.} Baseline, temporal module, and every
control share the frozen detections, the associator, and the evaluation code;
they differ only in the selection rule.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}
\noindent\textbf{Outdoor.} Official nuScenes mAP and NDS on the emitted box
set; class-agnostic HOTA, AssA, DetA, detection recall (DetRe), and IDF1 via
TrackEval~\cite{hota,idf1}, with similarity
$\mathrm{sim}=\mathrm{clip}(1-d_{xy}/2.0,\,0,\,1)$ matching the associator's
$2.0$\,m threshold; and official class-aware AMOTA~\cite{nuscenes_tracking}
over the $7$ nuScenes tracking classes. The class-agnostic and class-aware
families are reported side by side because they respond to different
properties of the stream.

\noindent\textbf{Indoor.} The primary endpoint is the per-scene
count of \emph{label switches}: frames in which a persistent instance's
emitted class label differs from its label in the previous frame \emph{in
which it was emitted}. Counting against the last emission rather than the
last frame is what makes the count comparable across arms, since the arms
emit on different frames. Prediction stability has been measured separately
from accuracy before --- as fragment, position, and scale errors for video
detection~\cite{vidstability}, and as a confidence/localisation stability
index for 3D detection~\cite{stability3d}: the former reports low correlation
between its stability metric and the accuracy metric, the latter that
stability ``cannot be accessed by existing metrics such as mAP and
MOTA'' --- but that family scores box trajectories against ground-truth
tracks, which ScanNet200 does not provide, and none of it scores the
\emph{class} assigned to a persistent instance. We therefore define the
switch count in that spirit for our setting. Because the arms also differ in
how many observations they emit, we report the count both as a total and as a
rate per switch opportunity, that is per emitted (instance, frame)
observation after each instance's first emission. The secondary endpoint is
instance-segmentation AP on the final reconstruction, with the expectation of
no material change.

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:stats}
For all three main comparisons --- detection-budget-matched,
identity-budget-matched, and indoor --- the arms, the primary endpoint, and
the criterion for a negative result were fixed before the run in a
pre-registration released with the code. For the outdoor comparisons that criterion was that the association
gain hold in \emph{both} association frames with scene-bootstrap intervals
excluding zero, and that the dataset-level metrics (mAP, AMOTA) move
consistently across the two association frames, whichever direction they move
in; indoors,
that the per-scene label-switch count fall. The causal-emission ablation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:emissionabl}) predates that protocol and is what motivated it. All comparisons between the
temporal module and a control are paired per scene. We report the per-scene Wilcoxon signed-rank test with the
rank-biserial correlation as effect size, and a $95\%$ bootstrap confidence
interval on the difference of the aggregate metric obtained by resampling
scenes ($10{,}000$ resamples, fixed seed $20260718$). A difference is described as an
improvement only when its interval excludes zero; intervals that span zero are
reported as no detectable difference, whatever their sign.

Intervals are available for the class-agnostic metrics, which are defined
per scene and aggregate linearly over scenes. AMOTA is not, and neither are
the nuScenes devkit metrics mAP and NDS: AMOTA integrates MOTA over recall
thresholds estimated on the whole split, and mAP and NDS are likewise computed
over the whole split, so a per-scene resample does not yield the same
estimator. We report all three as point estimates without intervals. We therefore do not claim interval support for
AMOTA anywhere; the evidence we offer for it is that its sign and rough
magnitude are stable across four independent comparisons (two controls
$\times$ two association frames).
