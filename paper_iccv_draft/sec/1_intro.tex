\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Streaming 3D perception systems --- detectors and semantic mappers that run on
a live sensor feed~\cite{streamingperception,streaming3d,conceptfusion,conceptgraphs}
--- are consumed downstream as \emph{persistent objects}: a map, a scene
graph, a planner's object memory. What those consumers need is that one
physical object stay one object with one label. What streaming systems
actually produce flickers. The prevailing emission rule is a per-frame
confidence threshold: every detection above a score cut-off enters the stream
immediately, independently of what was emitted a frame earlier. Nothing
carries an object's history forward, so a re-detected object arrives as a new
identity, and an unstable open-vocabulary label changes class along the way.
Per-frame detection
metrics do not see any of this, and so do not penalise it. Nor does the usual
demonstration of a temporal fix settle the matter: a component that suppresses
unstable output is conventionally compared against an unfiltered baseline, a
comparison that cannot separate what the component \emph{selects} from how
much it \emph{removes}. The property is thus both unmeasured by the dominant
metrics and unattributed by the dominant comparison.

We present a \emph{retrospective confirmation} module that improves identity
and semantic consistency in such a stream, and requires no retraining and no
change to the perception model. Detections are linked into tracks by a
class-agnostic associator; a track becomes \emph{confirmed} only after $N$
observations, and at that moment the module emits not only the current
detection but the track's withheld prefix, so a confirmed object's trajectory
is complete from its first observation. Nothing about a box's
coordinates, score, or label is modified. The retrospective step, which
ordinary online trackers~\cite{sort,deepsort,ab3dmot} do not perform, is what
makes the consistency gain hold in both of the association regimes we test,
and it is what the module contributes beyond the confirmation test it builds
on. The module runs at a single operating point ($N{=}3$) with no per-dataset
or per-metric tuning.

The attribution problem stated above applies to our module as much as to any
other: confirmation also changes the \emph{amount} of output, and every
volume-sensitive metric moves for that reason alone. We therefore evaluate it
under a protocol built to settle exactly that question --- non-temporal
\emph{matched controls}, which hold the output budget fixed so that only the
selection rule varies. A
\emph{detection-budget-matched} control thresholds the same frozen detector
output so that it emits exactly as many detections as the module; an
\emph{identity-budget-matched} control keeps, per sequence, the highest-scoring
$K$ tracks, where $K$ is the number of identities the module emits. Both
controls share the detector, the association step, and the evaluation code
with the module, and differ from it only in selecting by score instead of by
temporal persistence.

Under these controls, on the nuScenes validation set (150 scenes, a frozen
CenterPoint~\cite{centerpoint} detector, both sensor-frame and ego-motion-%
compensated world-frame association, confirmed tracks emitted
retrospectively), the module improves identity consistency and pays for it in
per-frame detection quality. It improves
association accuracy (HOTA's AssA component~\cite{hota}) over both matched
controls in both association frames, with bootstrap confidence intervals
excluding zero. The class-aware tracking accuracy
AMOTA~\cite{nuscenes_tracking}, which does not admit the same per-scene
resampling and is reported as a point estimate, rises consistently in sign and
magnitude across both controls and both frames. At the same output budget it
\emph{reduces} per-frame detection quality, a cost we quantify in
Sec.~\ref{sec:detmatch}. We present both sides as the finding: the benefit of
temporal confirmation is identity consistency, and it is bought rather than
added.

Because the module touches only emission timing, it transfers to any streaming
pipeline that re-observes objects. We apply the identical module, unchanged
and at the same $N$, to an indoor open-vocabulary instance-segmentation
pipeline that shares no detector, modality, or codebase with the outdoor one,
and evaluate it under the same control design. On ScanNet200 (312 validation
scenes, 3D instance-mask proposals labeled per frame by a 2D open-vocabulary
detector), the module reduces the number of frames in which an instance's
class label changes by 26.9\% relative to an identity-budget-matched control
of equal size, while leaving instance-segmentation AP essentially unchanged. The
temporal system has fewer label switches in 309 of 312 scenes and the paired
confidence interval excludes zero by a wide margin, whereas selecting the same
number of instances at random reproduces none of the reduction --- so the gain
is specific to temporal selection rather than to emitting fewer identities.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{A training-free retrospective confirmation module.} An operator
combining the standard confirmation test with retrospective emission of the
confirmed prefix, applied to frozen detector output at a single operating
point, with no retraining, no architectural change, and no modification of box
coordinates, scores, or labels.
\item \textbf{The retrospective emission policy, isolated.} We show the
retrospective step is what completes the module: emitting causally instead
narrows \emph{where} the gain holds --- it survives in the sensor frame but
reverses in the world frame --- and retrospection's price is $N{-}1$ frames
of latency ($1.0$\,s at $2$\,Hz for $N{=}3$).
\item \textbf{A matched-control evaluation protocol.} Two non-temporal
controls --- detection-budget-matched and identity-budget-matched --- with
paired per-sequence statistics, which separate what temporal selection
contributes from what merely emitting less contributes. The protocol is not
specific to confirmation and applies unchanged to any component that alters
output volume.
\item \textbf{Cross-domain validation under that protocol.} Outdoors, the
module improves association accuracy and class-aware tracking accuracy under
both controls in both association frames, at a detection cost we quantify in
Sec.~\ref{sec:detmatch} rather than leave implicit. Indoors, the identical module
reduces class-label switching on an unrelated open-vocabulary
instance-segmentation pipeline, with segmentation quality unchanged.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Streaming perception.} Streaming perception was formalised by
Li et al.~\cite{streamingperception}, who observed that a detector evaluated
offline is scored on a world that has already moved on, and introduced streaming
AP (sAP): predictions are compared against the state of the world at the moment
they are \emph{emitted}, so latency and accuracy are folded into one number.
Follow-up work optimises detectors directly for this
regime~\cite{streamyolo}, and the 3D analogue processes LiDAR
sectors as they arrive rather than waiting for a full sweep~\cite{streaming3d}.
This line establishes the premise we build on --- that a perception system on a
live stream must be judged as a stream --- but its correction is about
\emph{when} a prediction is scored. sAP is still a per-frame average precision:
two systems that emit identical boxes at identical latency but differ entirely
in identity continuity and label stability receive the same sAP. Our focus is
orthogonal and complementary: we hold latency and box geometry fixed and ask
what the per-frame family cannot see \emph{within} a frame-aligned comparison.

\noindent\textbf{Multi-object tracking.} Online tracking-by-detection descends
from SORT~\cite{sort}, which pairs a Kalman filter with greedy Hungarian IoU
association, and DeepSORT~\cite{deepsort}, which adds an appearance embedding to
survive occlusion. Both use a \emph{track-confirmation} rule: a tentative
track is not reported until it has been matched in $N$ consecutive frames. The
confirmation test is inherited from this literature; what has not been examined
is what it contributes once the volume it removes is held fixed. In 3D,
AB3DMOT~\cite{ab3dmot} showed that the same approach applied to 3D boxes is a
strong baseline, and CenterPoint~\cite{centerpoint} tracks by predicting
velocity and associating centre points greedily across frames. Throughout this
literature, confirmation is treated as a tuned nuisance parameter and compared
against an unfiltered tracker, so its measured benefit includes whatever
follows from emitting less output. We instead compare it against non-temporal
selection rules granted the same output budget, and report what changes on the
detection metrics the 3D perception field reports as well as on the tracking
metrics.

\noindent\textbf{Open-vocabulary 3D perception.} OpenScene~\cite{openscene}
distils 2D vision--language features into 3D points for zero-shot querying
without 3D annotation, and LERF~\cite{lerf} embeds language into a radiance
field. OpenMask3D~\cite{openmask3d} moves to instances, computing per-mask CLIP
features over class-agnostic 3D proposals, and Open-YOLO~3D~\cite{openyolo3d}
replaces the expensive per-mask feature extraction with cheap 2D open-vocabulary
box predictions projected onto 3D masks. All four are \emph{offline}: they
consume a completed reconstruction. ConceptFusion~\cite{conceptfusion} makes the
setting online, fusing open-set multimodal features into a map frame by frame,
and ConceptGraphs~\cite{conceptgraphs} builds an open-vocabulary 3D scene graph
incrementally, associating and merging per-frame detections into persistent
object nodes for planning. Once a system is incremental, \emph{which} per-frame
detection becomes which persistent object is a first-class question --- yet
evaluation remains per-scene AP or mIoU on the final map, which is insensitive
to how often a persistent object's identity or class label changes along the
way. Our indoor experiment measures exactly that property, on a pipeline of
this family, under a matched control.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation metrics.} CLEAR MOTA~\cite{clearmot} scores a
tracker by counting FP, FN, and identity switches, but is dominated by detection
errors; IDF1~\cite{idf1} rebalances toward identity by matching whole
trajectories one-to-one and reporting an identity F1. HOTA~\cite{hota} was
introduced precisely because these conflate two capabilities, and factorises the
score into a detection accuracy DetA and an association accuracy AssA, whose
geometric mean is HOTA. This factorisation is what makes our result legible:
association quality and detection quality can be read separately over an
\emph{identical} box set, which is precisely the distinction a temporal
selection rule acts on. In 3D, nuScenes AMOTA~\cite{nuscenes_tracking} ---
following AB3DMOT's integral formulation~\cite{ab3dmot} --- averages MOTA over
recall thresholds and is class-aware, so it responds to class labels that the
class-agnostic family ignores; we report both. Video consistency metrics
(STQ~\cite{step}, VPQ~\cite{vpq}, TETA~\cite{teta}) and open-vocabulary tracking
benchmarks (TAO~\cite{tao}, OVTrack~\cite{ovtrack}) extend the family but
likewise require dense GT tube identities over a fixed taxonomy. We use these
metrics where they are computable rather than proposing a new one; our
contribution is the controlled experiment and the protocol that reads it.

\noindent\textbf{Controlled evaluation of pipeline components.} Isolating what a
single component contributes requires holding the rest of the system fixed, and
for components that change how much output a system produces, holding the
output budget fixed as well; otherwise the comparison rewards volume rather than
the mechanism. Budget-matched comparison is standard practice when scoring
detectors at a fixed number of proposals, but it is not usually applied to
temporal components, whose effect on output volume is a side effect rather than
a parameter. The controls in this paper apply it in both of the currencies a
temporal component spends --- detections and identities --- and read the
resulting trade-off directly.
