\section{Retraction horizon}
\label{supp:horizon}
At $H{=}4$ a track is confirmed or retracted within five frames ($2.5$\,s) of
its last observation, instead of at scene end (up to $40$ frames, $20$\,s).
The bound costs nothing in output --- the emitted set is byte-identical to
$H{=}\infty$ --- and downstream event traffic is unchanged by it ($161.60$
events per frame at every $H$).

\section{Control ranking robustness}
\label{supp:ranking}
Ranking the identity-budget control by mean instead of peak refined score, or
matching identities dataset-wide instead of per scene, moves its AssA by less
than $0.007$ in both association frames.

\section{Confirmation latency distribution}
\label{supp:latency}
The $N{-}1$ figure quoted for retrospective emission is a count of
\emph{observations}. Over the $150$ scenes the confirmation \emph{frame}
horizon has mean $3.29$, median $3$, $95$th percentile $7$ and maximum $10$;
at the $2$\,Hz annotated-frame rate this is a median of $1.5$\,s with a tail of
$5.0$\,s, against a constant $2$-observation horizon. It is unbounded in
principle.

\section{Cross-detector stress test: BEVFusion-L}
\label{supp:crossdet}

\subsection*{Detector and protocol}
The body's tracking evaluation runs on one frozen CenterPoint detection set.
To test whether the association gain of Sec.~\ref{sec:detmatch} is a property
of the confirmation rule or of that detector, we repeat the same
budget-matched comparison on a second LiDAR detector: \textbf{BEVFusion-L},
LiDAR-only BEVFusion with a TransFusion head, at $\mathrm{mAP}=0.6435$ and
$\mathrm{NDS}=0.6918$ on nuScenes val. It is not a re-parameterisation of the
first: it is query-based and NMS-free, emitting a fixed $200$ queries per
sample, where CenterPoint is anchor-based with NMS in the loop. Proposal
density, score distribution and duplicate structure all differ, which is what
makes the swap informative.

Everything else is held fixed: nuScenes val, $150$ scenes, $6{,}019$ samples,
$N{=}3$, retrospective emission, both association frames, and the same matched
controls. Every control is a mask over one frozen box stream, so association,
geometry, scores and timestamps are shared by construction and only the
surviving set differs. The arm under test is \emph{confirmation}, so this
section bounds the premise of Sec.~\ref{sec:detmatch}; it does not evaluate the
provisional policy of Sec.~\ref{sec:provisional}, which we run on one detector
only.

\subsection*{Isolating emission from revision (both detectors)}
The confirmation module includes track-voted relabelling after emission
(Sec.~\ref{sec:pipeline}); in the world frame it overwrites $244{,}943$
emitted boxes. Its AMOTA therefore mixes the emission
decision with a semantic revision, while the accumulation controls only ever
change which boxes survive. We remove the confound with an arm that keeps the
confirmation arm's emission decision and restores the original per-box labels
--- same $858{,}279$ boxes, same $117{,}023$ identities, labels untouched.

Its class-agnostic metrics come back \emph{bit-identical} to the confirmation
arm (HOTA $0.2483$, AssA $0.4869$, DetA $0.1272$, IDF1 $0.2241$). This is
mechanical, not a null result: those metrics are computed by a class-agnostic
matcher and cannot respond to a label change, the same invariance noted in
Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}. It also means the world-frame AssA result of
Tab.~\ref{tab:bevtransfer} is unaffected by the relabelling.

AMOTA does respond: it falls from $0.3121$ to $0.2793$, and AMOTP rises from
$0.8781$ to $0.9448$. So $0.0328$ of the confirmation arm's world-frame AMOTA
margin was the revision rather than the emission decision. Tab.~\ref{tab:bevtransfer}
reports the emission-only figures.

\subsection*{Detection cost}
The cost the body reports is larger on this detector. In the sensor frame
confirmation takes mAP from $0.6435$ to $0.3500$ and NDS from $0.6918$ to
$0.5497$, while the threshold control spending the identical box budget holds
mAP at $0.6423$ and NDS at $0.6918$. In the world frame the same policy takes
mAP to $0.5706$ and NDS to $0.6610$ against a control that gives up nothing.
The association gain, where it exists at all, is bought at a detection price
that a matched non-temporal rule does not pay.

\subsection*{What this section does and does not establish}
$\Delta$AMOTA carries no confidence interval, for the reason given in
Sec.~\ref{sec:stats}: AMOTA integrates MOTA over recall thresholds estimated on
the whole split and has no per-scene decomposition, so the scene-resample
protocol does not yield the same estimator. Producing one would require
re-running the official evaluation once per resample, which is a different
protocol from the one fixed before the run; we did not do it. The AMOTA pattern here
is therefore a replicated point estimate --- positive in all six cells of
Tab.~\ref{tab:bevtransfer} and in the CenterPoint cells of the body --- and not
a statistically bounded effect.

Two further limits. DetRe is not reported for this detector, so
Tab.~\ref{tab:bevphase1} is not directly comparable to
Tab.~\ref{tab:detmatch} column for column. And this is one additional detector:
it is enough to refute a detector-independent reading of the association gain,
which is what we use it for, and not enough to characterise how the gain varies
with detector design.
