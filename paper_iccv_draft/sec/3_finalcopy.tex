\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We report three outdoor comparisons on nuScenes val and one indoor comparison
on ScanNet200 val, each between the confirmation-based module and a control
granted the same output budget over shared frozen detections. Unless stated
otherwise, outdoor results use retrospective emission, whose latency cost is
$N{-}1$ frames ($1.0$\,s at $2$\,Hz).

Table~\ref{tab:detmatch} compares confirmation against controls calibrated to
the identical box count, so the comparison is free of the volume confound: all
matched arms emit $360{,}309$ boxes in the sensor frame and $754{,}500$ in the
world frame, from the same frozen detector output.

\noindent\textbf{Association accuracy improves.} Against the threshold control
AssA rises from $0.2044$ to $0.2535$ in the sensor frame and from $0.4175$ to
$0.4314$ in the world frame, both intervals excluding zero
($[{+}0.0370,{+}0.0615]$ and $[{+}0.0086,{+}0.0195]$). Per scene the
sensor-frame gain averages $+0.0340$ --- a different estimator from the
$0.0491$ difference of aggregates --- and holds in $110$ of $150$ scenes
(Wilcoxon $p{=}4.6{\times}10^{-13}$, rank-biserial $0.68$).

\noindent\textbf{Class-aware tracking accuracy rises.} AMOTA goes from
$0.0547$ to $0.0887$ (sensor) and $0.1655$ to $0.2033$ (world), the largest
relative effect we measure. AMOTA does not admit the per-scene resampling used
for the other metrics (Sec.~\ref{sec:stats}), so this is a point estimate; what
we rely on is that it moves the same way in both frames and under the
comparisons below.

\noindent\textbf{Detection-oriented metrics fall.} At the same budget, mAP
drops $0.3324\!\to\!0.2023$ (sensor) and $0.3396\!\to\!0.2900$ (world), DetA
drops $0.2184\!\to\!0.1537$ ($[{-}0.0700,{-}0.0593]$) and
$0.1282\!\to\!0.1196$ ($[{-}0.0101,{-}0.0072]$), and detection recall drops
$0.5182\!\to\!0.3859$ ($[{-}0.1483,{-}0.1170]$) and $0.5668\!\to\!0.5326$
($[{-}0.0404,{-}0.0286]$); mAP is a dataset-level metric and is reported as a
point estimate. Because HOTA is the geometric mean of DetA
and AssA, the DetA loss dominates and aggregate HOTA falls as well
($[{-}0.0180,{-}0.0103]$ sensor; $[{-}0.0059,{-}0.0027]$ world). The trade-off
is explicit: the module buys association quality with detection quality rather
than adding it on top.

\noindent\textbf{IDF1 shows no detectable sensor-frame difference.} The
sensor-frame IDF1 interval spans zero ($[{-}0.0046,{+}0.0064]$; paired
$p{=}0.45$, $77/150$ scenes), so we report no difference there. In the world
frame IDF1 is slightly \emph{lower} for the confirmation module
($[{-}0.0091,{-}0.0047]$).

\noindent\textbf{Against confidence accumulation, the two rules split the
metrics.} The accumulation control emits the same boxes and is the comparison
that isolates \emph{which} temporal rule is used rather than whether one is
used at all. Confirmation attains higher AssA in both frames
($0.2217\!\to\!0.2535$ sensor, $0.4190\!\to\!0.4314$ world; intervals
$[{+}0.0249,{+}0.0388]$ and $[{+}0.0089,{+}0.0163]$), and accumulation is ahead
on every other measured axis, also with intervals excluding zero: HOTA
($[{-}0.0084,{-}0.0068]$ sensor, $[{-}0.0049,{-}0.0039]$ world), DetA
($[{-}0.0393,{-}0.0315]$, $[{-}0.0092,{-}0.0074]$), IDF1
($[{-}0.0076,{-}0.0054]$, $[{-}0.0099,{-}0.0084]$) and DetRe. Neither rule
dominates: they occupy different points of the association--detection
trade-off, and the choice between them is a choice of which axis to spend.
Because mAP was not computed for this control, we make no mAP-based claim
about it.

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the accumulation control.} In the world frame this
control is not statistically separable from the static threshold on any of the
five metrics we resample (all intervals span zero); in the sensor frame it is.
This concerns our reproduction under the frozen association and
track-management configuration stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}, where the gap
tolerance is our associator's, and is not a claim about the original system or
its published results.

Matching the number of emitted identities rather than the number of boxes
(Tab.~\ref{tab:idmatch}) tests whether the association gain merely reflects
having fewer identities to confuse. It does not. AssA remains higher for the
confirmation module in both frames ($0.2346\!\to\!0.2535$ sensor,
$0.4173\!\to\!0.4314$ world), with intervals excluding zero
($[{+}0.0129,{+}0.0258]$ and $[{+}0.0101,{+}0.0185]$) and per-scene wins in
$117/150$ and $111/150$ scenes ($p{=}2.2{\times}10^{-16}$ and
$4.1{\times}10^{-11}$). Selecting the same number of tracks \emph{at random}
rather than by score (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}) reproduces none of it: over
the five seeded arms AssA reaches at most $0.2460$ (sensor) and $0.3934$
(world), both below the confirmation module (Tab.~\ref{tab:idmatch}). AMOTA again favours confirmation ($0.0547\!\to\!0.0887$
sensor, $0.1587\!\to\!0.2033$ world).

\noindent\textbf{The rule separation survives identity matching.} Repeating the
comparison against confidence accumulation with \emph{identities} rather than
boxes held fixed --- the accumulation control ranked by its own refined score
and cut at the confirmation module's per-scene identity count, matched exactly
--- leaves the association separation unchanged: $+0.0252$ sensor
($[{+}0.0200,{+}0.0316]$) and $+0.0172$ world ($[{+}0.0089,{+}0.0161]$),
against $+0.0253$ and $+0.0170$ at matched boxes. The two matchings agree to
within $0.0003$ in both frames, and the accompanying losses on HOTA, DetA,
IDF1 and DetRe keep their sign and significance. Whichever currency is held
fixed, then, the separation between the two rules is the same size, which
excludes the simplest reading of it --- that persistence gains association
accuracy by carrying fewer identities. Ranking the control by mean instead of
peak refined score, or matching identities dataset-wide instead of per scene,
moves its AssA by less than $0.004$, so the result is not an artifact of that
choice. We report no AMOTA comparison under identity matching: the count of
identities entering the class-aware evaluation is not equalised by matching the
emitted count (Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\noindent\textbf{Budget asymmetry.} The two controls cannot be satisfied
simultaneously. At equal identity count the control emits fewer boxes than the
confirmation module ($190{,}708$ vs.\ $360{,}309$ sensor; $608{,}168$ vs.\
$754{,}500$ world), because score-ranked tracks are shorter than
persistence-selected ones. This works \emph{against} the control on
volume-sensitive metrics, so the AssA and AMOTA results here are conservative;
conversely the control's advantage on DetA and HOTA in this table is partly a
budget artifact and should be read from Tab.~\ref{tab:detmatch}, where boxes
are matched exactly. IDF1 likewise favours the control here
(Tab.~\ref{tab:idmatch}), which the control's shorter tracks help; we read it
together with Tab.~\ref{tab:detmatch}, where it shows no sensor-frame
difference. The two tables are meant to be read together: the
association gain survives both matchings, and the detection loss is
established under the matching that isolates it.

The results above emit a confirmed track's withheld prefix retrospectively,
which costs $1.0$\,s of latency. Table~\ref{tab:emission} reports the causal
alternative, in which the prefix is permanently dropped, again against a
count-matched score threshold. The result splits by association frame. The
AMOTA gain survives in both frames ($0.0574\!\to\!0.0800$ sensor,
$0.1825\!\to\!0.2089$ world), and in the sensor frame the association gain is
not merely preserved but larger than under retrospective emission
($0.2105\!\to\!0.2861$; interval $[{+}0.0577,{+}0.0922]$ against
$[{+}0.0370,{+}0.0615]$ retrospectively, $110$ of $150$ scenes). In the world
frame, however, causal emission \emph{loses} association accuracy relative to
the control ($0.4315\!\to\!0.4199$), with a bootstrap interval entirely below
zero ($[{-}0.0183,{-}0.0047]$; paired $p{=}0.008$, rank-biserial $-0.25$, with
confirmation ahead in only $53$ of $150$ scenes). The detection cost is larger
throughout than under retrospective emission (mAP $0.3213\!\to\!0.1360$
sensor).

The latency-free policy is therefore not uniformly worse --- it is worse where
association was already easy. Ego-motion compensation removes most apparent
motion between frames, so the world-frame control associates well on its own
(AssA $0.4315$ against $0.2105$ in the sensor frame), and there the detections
lost with the discarded prefix outweigh what confirmation cleans up. We offer
that reading as interpretation; the operational consequence stands on the
measurements alone: a system that cannot absorb $N{-}1$ frames of latency can
still use confirmation, and should expect the association gain only if it
associates in sensor coordinates.

\subsection{Confirmation-window sensitivity}
\label{sec:nsweep}

\input{figs/fig_nsweep_tradeoff_body}

All results so far fix $N{=}3$. Figure~\ref{fig:nsweep} varies it over
$\{2,3,4,5\}$, giving each $N$ its own exactly matched control, and asks what
the choice is worth. $N{=}1$ is excluded: it confirms every track on its first
observation, reproduces the unfiltered baseline detection-for-detection, and
its matched control is therefore the same box set, so every difference is zero
by construction. We use it only as an implementation check.

The association gain does not depend on the operating point. It is positive at
every $N$ in both association frames, and all eight intervals exclude zero. It
also grows monotonically with $N$ --- in the sensor frame close to linearly
($+0.0219$, $+0.0491$, $+0.0767$, $+0.1038$), in the world frame with visible
deceleration ($+0.0058$, $+0.0139$, $+0.0184$, $+0.0220$). The class-aware
metric behaves differently: AMOTA flattens in the sensor frame
($+0.0393\!\to\!+0.0414$ from $N{=}4$ to $N{=}5$) and in the world frame turns
over, peaking at $N{=}4$ ($+0.0424$) and falling at $N{=}5$ ($+0.0381$). The
detection cost, meanwhile, rises without turning over at any $N$ we measured,
and the latency rises linearly by construction.

No single $N$ in this range is best on every metric, and $N{=}3$ is not the
best on any of them. We report this rather than adjust the operating point:
re-selecting $N$ from the sweep would convert an inherited constant into a
value tuned on the test metrics, the practice the matched controls exist to
guard against. We keep $N{=}3$ because $N$ is a latency budget, not a tuned
hyperparameter: it is fixed by the $N{-}1$ frames of delay a deployment can
absorb, and the role of the sweep is to price that budget --- what each
additional frame of delay buys in association and costs in detection. What the sweep establishes is that the consistency gain is a
property of confirmation over the whole range tested, not an artifact of one
setting, and that larger windows buy more of it at proportionally more
detection cost and latency. The widening gap is driven by the confirmed arm
rather than by a failing control, whose own AssA also rises as the matched
budget tightens ($0.1980\!\to\!0.2107$ sensor, $0.4131\!\to\!0.4262$ world).

The indoor experiment asks whether the same rule improves \emph{semantic}
stability in a pipeline that shares no detector, modality, or codebase with
the outdoor one. Table~\ref{tab:indoor} reports the primary endpoint. Against the identity-matched control, confirmation reduces label
switches from $23{,}303$ to $17{,}045$, a $26.9\%$ reduction at an essentially
equal instance budget ($10{,}413$ vs.\ $10{,}410$). Per scene the reduction is
$18.0$ switches at the median ($20.1$ on average, $95\%$ CI on the mean
$[18.7, 21.5]$) and holds in $309$ of $312$ scenes (Wilcoxon
$p{=}1.5{\times}10^{-52}$, rank-biserial $0.99$).

The three random-$K$ arms ($23{,}275$, $23{,}277$, $23{,}288$ switches) are
indistinguishable from the score-ranked control and far from the confirmation
module, so the reduction is specific to temporal selection rather than to
emitting fewer instances by any rule.

The secondary endpoint behaves as expected: instance
segmentation AP is $0.1954$ against $0.1956$ for the unfiltered baseline and
$0.1926$ for the control, i.e.\ no material change. Unlike the outdoor
setting, the indoor gain therefore does not come at a measurable
detection-quality cost --- the two pipelines differ in whether the withheld
detections carry segmentation mass, and we report the difference in effect
without claiming the outdoor trade-off is avoidable in general.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{What the module improves, and what the controls add.}
Compared against an unfiltered baseline, the module appears to improve nearly
every tracking metric. Compared against a non-temporal rule with the same
output budget, part of that apparent improvement turns out to be the effect of
emitting less, and what remains is a sharper claim: association accuracy and
class-aware tracking accuracy improve, detection-oriented accuracy degrades,
and identity F1 is unchanged at a matched detection budget but lower at a
matched identity budget, where the control's shorter tracks favour it. Against
the other temporal rule the picture is not an ordering but a split: confidence
accumulation keeps the detection- and identity-oriented measures, persistence
takes association accuracy, and both halves of that statement have intervals
excluding zero under either budget. The controls are what let us say any of
this; they are the evidence standard behind the claim rather than a separate
finding, and the general lesson --- a component that spends output volume must
be compared at fixed volume, including against other components that spend it
--- is available to anyone evaluating a similar module.

\noindent\textbf{Association and detection respond to opposite things.} What
a confirmation rule removes is fixed by construction: tracks observed fewer
than $N$ times. The falling detection recall shows that some of those withheld
detections matched ground truth, which is the cost side. The gain side admits
an interpretation we can offer but did not measure separately --- that the
identities surviving confirmation are the ones an associator handles most
reliably. What the measurements establish is that the two halves move in
opposite directions over an identical box set, which is why we report DetA and
AssA rather than HOTA alone: the aggregate hides a trade-off it averages over.

\noindent\textbf{The two domains agree in direction, not in cost.} Outdoors
the identity gain is paid for in detection quality; indoors the analogous
semantic-stability gain leaves AP unchanged. In both pipelines temporal
selection improves a property the per-frame metric does not measure --- identity
consistency outdoors, label stability indoors --- across two systems sharing
nothing but the rule. The differing cost is a property of the pipelines, and we
do not generalise from the indoor result that the outdoor cost is avoidable.

\noindent\textbf{Practical reading.} Within the settings we measured, the case
our evidence favours is a system whose output is consumed as persistent
objects: a map, a scene graph, a planner's object memory. A system scored on
per-frame detection quality should not adopt confirmation-based initialization,
and one that needs the detection- and identity-oriented measures is better
served by accumulating confidence than by counting observations. Latency
decides not whether to adopt confirmation but what to expect: with $N{-}1$
frames of slack the association gain holds in both frames, and without it only
in the sensor frame (Sec.~\ref{sec:emissionabl}).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{One tracking-GT dataset.} The full tracking evaluation (HOTA family,
AMOTA) is nuScenes only. ScanNet200 has no instance-tracking ground truth, so
the indoor endpoint is label switching rather than an identity metric, and the
two domains are not measured with the same instrument. Multi-dataset tracking
validation (Waymo, Argoverse) remains future work.

\textbf{One operating point.} We fix $N{=}3$ and do not tune it per dataset or
metric. The value is inherited: it is the conventional default in
tracking-by-detection systems~\cite{sort,deepsort}, not a setting we chose by
inspecting our own results, and the same value is used in both domains. This
avoids selection on the test metric. The outdoor sweep of
Sec.~\ref{sec:nsweep} shows the association gain holds across
$N\in\{2,3,4,5\}$, so the claim is not specific to $N{=}3$; it also shows
$N{=}3$ is not the best setting on any single metric, and that the class-aware
metric turns over inside the range while the class-agnostic one does not. $N$
is therefore chosen as a latency budget rather than tuned. We did not sweep $N$
indoors, and we do not know where either curve ends beyond $N{=}5$.

\textbf{Budget matching is not simultaneous.} Matching detections leaves
identity counts unequal and vice versa (Sec.~\ref{sec:idmatch}). We report
both matchings and their directions of bias rather than a single comparison
that is matched in both currencies, which does not exist for a rule that
changes track lengths. Matching emitted identities also does not equalise the
identities that reach the class-aware evaluation, which scores a subset of
classes: at an identical emitted count the accumulation control carries
$13\%$ more identities into that evaluation in the world frame. We therefore
restrict the identity-matched comparison to the class-agnostic metrics and
report AMOTA only under detection matching.

\textbf{One reproduced comparator.} The confidence-accumulation control is our
reimplementation, run inside our frozen association with our gap tolerance and
without the comparator's trained score variant. It is a faithful control for
the mechanism, not a reproduction of a published system, and no statement here
should be read as characterising that system's own performance. mAP was not
computed for this control, so the detection-side comparison against it rests on
DetA and DetRe.

\textbf{Latency.} All main results use retrospective emission and are
therefore not achievable online at zero latency. The causal variant is
measured (Sec.~\ref{sec:emissionabl}) and preserves the association gain only
in the sensor frame.

\textbf{Frozen detector.} Every arm replays one stored detection set, which is
what makes the comparison clean, but it also means we characterise the rule's
effect for a single detector's error profile. A detector with fewer
short-lived false positives has less for confirmation to remove.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

Because the causal variant forfeits the world-frame association gain and the
retrospective one buys it with $N{-}1$ frames of latency, the interesting
design space is between them: a policy that emits provisionally and revises, or
that varies confirmation depth with association confidence, would trade latency
against identity consistency continuously rather than at the two endpoints we
measure. A rule combining persistence with accumulated confidence is the
natural companion question, given that the two split the metrics here. The
controls of Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol} support these measurements unmodified, as
they do a stronger associator or a different detector.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a training-free retrospective confirmation module for streaming
3D perception: detections are linked into tracks, a track enters the output
stream only after $N$ observations, and its withheld prefix is then emitted
retrospectively. It requires no retraining, leaves box coordinates, scores,
and labels untouched, and runs at one operating point across two pipelines
that share nothing else. On nuScenes with a frozen detector it improves
association accuracy and class-aware tracking accuracy in both association
frames; on ScanNet200 it reduces class-label switching by $26.9\%$ with
segmentation AP unchanged. These results are established against controls
granted the module's own output budget --- in detections and in identities ---
so what they credit is temporal selection rather than the smaller volume it
emits. The gain is bought rather than added: at an equal detection budget the
module trades away per-frame detection quality (Sec.~\ref{sec:detmatch}), and
the retrospective step costs $N{-}1$ frames of latency
(Sec.~\ref{sec:emissionabl}). Held against the other established temporal rule
at the same budgets, confirmation does not dominate it: accumulating confidence
