\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

We report three outdoor comparisons on nuScenes val and one indoor comparison
on ScanNet200 val, each between the confirmation-based module and a control
granted the same output budget over shared frozen detections, and measure what
the provisional stream is worth to a latency-sensitive consumer
(Sec.~\ref{sec:consumer}). Unless stated
otherwise, outdoor results use retrospective emission, whose latency cost is
$N{-}1$ observations; the corresponding frame-index delay is reported in
Sec.~\ref{sec:emissionabl}.

Table~\ref{tab:detmatch} compares confirmation against controls calibrated to
the identical box count --- $257{,}259$ boxes in the sensor frame and
$517{,}566$ in the world frame, from the same frozen detector output.

\noindent\textbf{Association accuracy improves.} Against the threshold control
AssA rises from $0.2119$ to $0.2744$ in the sensor frame and from $0.5146$ to
$0.5293$ in the world frame, both intervals excluding zero
($[{+}0.0471,{+}0.0785]$ and $[{+}0.0098,{+}0.0203]$). The per-scene mean gain, a different estimator
from the difference of aggregates, is $+0.0460$; the gain holds in $113$ of
$150$ scenes
(Wilcoxon $p{=}2.6{\times}10^{-15}$, rank-biserial $0.74$).

\noindent\textbf{Class-aware tracking accuracy rises.} AMOTA goes from
$0.0942$ to $0.1338$ (sensor) and $0.3137$ to $0.3864$ (world). AMOTA admits no per-scene resampling
(Sec.~\ref{sec:stats}); this is a point estimate.

\noindent\textbf{Detection-oriented metrics fall.} At the same budget, mAP
drops $0.5504\!\to\!0.3009$ (sensor) and $0.5598\!\to\!0.4921$ (world), DetA
drops $0.3570\!\to\!0.2232$ ($[{-}0.1408,{-}0.1267]$) and
$0.2133\!\to\!0.1973$ ($[{-}0.0183,{-}0.0138]$), and detection recall drops
$0.6160\!\to\!0.4287$ ($[{-}0.2087,{-}0.1665]$) and $0.6550\!\to\!0.6141$
($[{-}0.0477,{-}0.0350]$). The DetA loss dominates the geometric mean, so
aggregate HOTA falls as well ($[{-}0.0323,{-}0.0231]$ sensor;
$[{-}0.0100,{-}0.0063]$ world).

\noindent\textbf{IDF1 shows no reliable sensor-frame difference.} The
sensor-frame interval spans zero ($[{-}0.0108,{+}0.0037]$) while the paired
per-scene test reaches significance ($p{=}7.3{\times}10^{-3}$), so the two
estimators disagree and we claim no difference; in the world frame IDF1 is
slightly \emph{lower} for the confirmation module
($[{-}0.0135,{-}0.0070]$).

\noindent\textbf{Against confidence accumulation, the two rules split the
metrics.} This control isolates \emph{which} temporal rule is used rather
than whether one is used at all. Confirmation attains higher AssA in both
frames
($0.2315\!\to\!0.2744$ sensor, $0.5110\!\to\!0.5293$ world; intervals
$[{+}0.0337,{+}0.0524]$ and $[{+}0.0146,{+}0.0225]$), and accumulation is ahead
on every other measured axis, also with intervals excluding zero: HOTA, DetA, IDF1 and DetRe
(Sec.~\ref{supp:cbmot}).
The split extends to detection quality: accumulation leads on mAP
($0.4783\!\to\!0.3009$ sensor, $0.5568\!\to\!0.4921$ world) and NDS
($0.6134\!\to\!0.5229$, $0.6467\!\to\!0.6200$).

\noindent\textbf{Scope of the accumulation control.} All statements about it
concern our reproduction under the configuration of Sec.~\ref{sec:controls},
not the original system or its published results
(Sec.~\ref{supp:cbmot}).

Matching emitted identities rather than boxes
(Tab.~\ref{tab:idmatch}) tests whether the association gain merely reflects
having fewer identities to confuse. It does not. AssA remains higher for the
confirmation module in both frames ($0.2607\!\to\!0.2744$ sensor,
$0.5074\!\to\!0.5293$ world), with intervals excluding zero
($[{+}0.0071,{+}0.0216]$ and $[{+}0.0177,{+}0.0267]$) and per-scene wins in
$107/150$ and $116/150$ scenes ($p{=}3.9{\times}10^{-15}$ and
$2.9{\times}10^{-16}$). Selecting the same number of tracks \emph{at random}
rather than by score does not reproduce the world-frame result: the seeded arms
reach at most $0.4776$, well below the confirmation module's $0.5293$. In the
sensor frame the random arms are \emph{not} separated from it --- they span
$0.2595$--$0.2765$ and straddle the confirmation module's $0.2744$ --- so the
sensor-frame identity result rests on the score-ranked control alone.

\noindent\textbf{The rule separation survives identity matching.} Repeating the
comparison against confidence accumulation with \emph{identities} rather than
boxes held fixed --- the accumulation control ranked by its own whole-track peak refined score
and cut at the confirmation module's per-scene identity count, matched exactly
--- leaves the association separation unchanged. The paired per-scene mean
$\Delta$AssA is $+0.0327$ sensor $[{+}0.0262,{+}0.0397]$ and $+0.0244$ world
$[{+}0.0189,{+}0.0301]$, both intervals excluding zero. The same per-scene mean
estimator at matched boxes gives $+0.0341$ $[{+}0.0273,{+}0.0414]$ and
$+0.0251$ $[{+}0.0196,{+}0.0310]$, with
the accompanying losses
on HOTA, DetA, IDF1 and DetRe keeping their sign and significance. The
separation is similar in magnitude under either matched-budget constraint, which excludes
the simplest reading of it --- that persistence gains association accuracy by
carrying fewer identities. The result is robust to the control's ranking and
matching granularity (Sec.~\ref{supp:ranking}).

\noindent\textbf{Budget asymmetry.} Identity matching necessarily leaves
unequal box counts, because score-ranked tracks are shorter than
persistence-selected ones. This biases volume-sensitive metrics against the
control --- so the AssA result here is conservative --- and we therefore read
DetA, HOTA and IDF1 from Tab.~\ref{tab:detmatch}, where boxes are matched
exactly.

\subsection{Confirmation-window sensitivity}
\label{sec:nsweep}

All results so far fix $N{=}3$. Table~\ref{tab:nsweep} (supplementary) varies it over
$\{2,3,4,5\}$, each against its own exactly matched control ($N{=}1$ is
excluded: it reproduces the unfiltered baseline detection-for-detection, so
every difference is zero by construction). The association gain is positive at
every $N$ in both frames and all eight intervals exclude zero. It grows
monotonically with $N$, close to linearly in the sensor frame and with visible
deceleration in the world frame. AMOTA differs by frame: it keeps rising
through $N{=}5$ in the sensor frame but peaks at $N{=}4$ in the world frame.
The detection cost rises without
turning over at any $N$ we measured.

No single $N$ is best on every metric, and $N{=}3$ is best on none of them. We
report this rather than re-select: tuning $N$ on the test metrics is the
practice the matched controls exist to guard against, and $N$ is a latency
budget --- the $N{-}1$ observations of delay a deployment can absorb --- not a
hyperparameter. What the sweep establishes is that the consistency gain is a
property of confirmation over the whole range tested, and that larger windows
buy more of it at proportionally more detection cost and latency.

\subsection{Policy behaviour and its trade-off}
\label{sec:emissionabl}

Sections~\ref{sec:detmatch} and~\ref{sec:idmatch} price confirmation in
detection retention. This section asks what \emph{else} it pays. All results so
far release a confirmed track's withheld prefix retrospectively; we first
measure the causal alternative, then a policy that avoids both costs. Two things
change here: these arms are not budget-matched, since the emitted box count is what an emission policy acts on, and the comparator is the confirmation test
alone, without the module's relabelling and merging.

\noindent\textbf{Causal emission loses the prefix, and with it the world-frame
gain.} Table~\ref{tab:causal} repeats the detection-budget-matched comparison
with the prefix permanently dropped. The result splits by association frame.
The AMOTA gain survives in both ($0.0997\!\to\!0.1290$ sensor,
$0.3417\!\to\!0.4050$ world), and in the sensor frame the association gain is
not merely preserved but larger than under retrospective emission
($0.2147\!\to\!0.3088$; interval $[{+}0.0722,{+}0.1162]$ against
$[{+}0.0471,{+}0.0785]$ retrospectively, $112$ of $150$ scenes). In the world
frame, however, causal emission \emph{loses} association accuracy relative to
the control ($0.5308\!\to\!0.5184$), with a bootstrap interval entirely below
zero ($[{-}0.0195,{-}0.0049]$; paired $p{=}0.036$, rank-biserial $-0.20$,
confirmation ahead in only $59$ of $150$ scenes). The detection cost is larger
throughout than under retrospective emission (mAP $0.5287\!\to\!0.2047$
sensor).

Causal emission is worse where association was already easy: ego-motion
compensation leaves the world-frame control associating well on its own, and
there the discarded prefix costs more than confirmation cleans up.

\noindent\textbf{How much latency, in which unit.} The $N-1$ figure routinely
quoted for retrospective emission is correct only in observations
(Sec.~\ref{sec:provisional}): over the $150$ scenes the confirmation \emph{frame}
horizon has median $3$ and $95$th percentile $7$ --- $1.5$\,s and $3.5$\,s at
the $2$\,Hz annotated-frame rate, against a constant $2$-observation horizon.
It is unbounded in principle (Sec.~\ref{supp:latency}).

\noindent\textbf{Provisional emission retains the prefix at zero latency.}
Table~\ref{tab:policy} measures the proposed policy against causal emission
under the $N{=}3$ confirmation test alone --- the prefix-deleting comparator,
which applies neither the relabelling nor the merging of the confirmation
module (Sec.~\ref{sec:pipeline}) --- over the identical frozen stream. At a retraction
horizon of $H{=}4$ frames the policy emits $529{,}662$ boxes against that arm's
$368{,}724$: it retains $160{,}938$ observations ($+43.6\%$) that the arm
deletes, and it does so at an emission latency of exactly zero frames. Detection
quality on the emitted set rises accordingly (mAP $0.4483\!\to\!0.4927$, NDS
$0.6013\!\to\!0.6206$) and so does association consistency
(AssA $0.5265\!\to\!0.5378$); that same final volume selected by score alone
($529{,}662$ boxes, own association) reaches only $0.5132$
($+0.0246$, $[{+}0.0198,{+}0.0300]$), so this final-state figure is not a
volume effect.

\noindent\textbf{And it is not free.} Over the same comparison the policy
gives up class-agnostic detection and identity quality: DetA
$0.2275\!\to\!0.1936$, aggregate HOTA $0.3452\!\to\!0.3219$, IDF1
$0.3662\!\to\!0.3305$, and AMOTA before revision falls
$0.4051\!\to\!0.3823$. Emitting a provisional record means emitting records
that the prefix-deleting comparator would have suppressed and that ground truth does not support, and
the class-agnostic metrics price exactly that.

\noindent\textbf{Revision recovers the class-aware endpoint.} Applying the
completed-evidence label to the already-emitted stream takes AMOTA
$0.3823\!\to\!0.4646$ at $H{=}4$ ($+0.0823$), leaving the net effect against
the prefix-deleting comparator at $+0.0594$: revision restores, and then
exceeds, an AMOTA the emission step had lost rather than adding one on top of
it. It says nothing about association --- the class-agnostic matcher does not
read the class label, so those columns are a mechanical identity, not a null
result.

\noindent\textbf{The retraction horizon saturates.} $H$ prices how long a
provisional record may still be withdrawn. At $H{=}4$ the emitted output
becomes identical --- byte-identical, verified by checksum --- to the
unbounded $H{=}\infty$ policy that defers the decision to scene end, because no
track that ever confirms has a pre-confirmation gap wider than four frames.
Every endpoint at $H\!\ge\!4$ therefore coincides by output equality rather
than by a tuned metric match. The bound replaces the scene-end decision at no cost
in output (Sec.~\ref{supp:horizon}).

\noindent\textbf{The horizon is itself a trade-off.} Tightening it to $H{=}0$
--- retract at the first missed frame --- reverses the picture: the policy then
emits fewer boxes than the prefix-deleting comparator ($295{,}403$) and is the \emph{best} arm in the
table on every class-agnostic metric (AssA $0.5619$, HOTA $0.3675$, DetA
$0.2417$, IDF1 $0.4198$), while giving up mAP and AMOTA. We report both
endpoints rather than selecting the one that flatters a chosen column: $H$
buys retention and detection quality with class-agnostic identity quality,
and $H{=}4$ is the setting at which the unbounded-horizon result is recovered
in full at bounded latency.

Zero emission latency is a property of the stream; whether it is worth
anything depends on what reads it. We measure it with the simplest consumer
for which timing is the whole question: an object memory that must hold each
ground-truth object at the frame the object first comes within $R{=}20$\,m of
the ego vehicle. The memory applies every emitted record, revision and
retraction as it arrives; an object is \emph{missed} if no held record is
assigned to it at that frame under one-to-one matching at the associator's
$2.0$\,m threshold, regardless of class, and a held record within $R$ assigned to no object is a \emph{false
alert}. The $5{,}487$ critical objects are defined from ground truth and ego
pose alone, before any arm is read, and $R$ lies inside the smallest official
per-class evaluation range ($30$\,m), so no class is truncated.

\noindent\textbf{The prefix-deleting arm is still waiting when it matters.}
Against the causal comparator the policy cuts the miss rate by $67.4$ points in
the sensor frame ($[{+}64.1,{+}70.6]$, fewer misses in all $149$ scenes that
contain a critical object) and $45.7$ points in the world frame
($[{+}41.6,{+}49.9]$, $147$ of $149$; Tab.~\ref{tab:consumer}). These are not
late arrivals: $1{,}725$ of the comparator's $3{,}262$ sensor-frame misses are
never held before the object leaves the $20$\,m radius. The reduction keeps its
sign with objects visible from a scene's first frame excluded, at a $1.0$\,m
matching distance, and at $R{=}10$ and $30$\,m (Tab.~\ref{tab:consumer-robust}).

\noindent\textbf{The consumer pays for it.} The same memory holds far more
records that correspond to nothing: about $95$ additional false-alert frames
per recovered object in the sensor frame ($60$ in the world frame). Against the
score-ranked control the policy misses fewer
objects in the sensor frame ($[{+}9.7,{+}14.1]$ points) at nearly four times
its false alerts, and in the world frame the two miss rates show no detectable
difference ($[{-}0.02,{+}0.67]$ points, $p{=}0.078$) while the control raises
fewer false alerts. The advantage measured here is over waiting for
confirmation; superiority over immediate emission is not established. A consumer that instead admits only
confirmed records holds exactly the comparator's memory, so the two arms are
the endpoints of consumer policy rather than two points on a curve. Waiting
cost is converted into correction cost, not removed. Revision's share of the
correction is narrow: in the world frame it cuts label reversals on critical
objects from $1{,}296$ to $887$, and it changes no entry of
Tab.~\ref{tab:consumer}.

The indoor experiment asks whether the same rule improves \emph{semantic}
stability in the pipeline of Sec.~\ref{sec:datasets}, which shares nothing with
the outdoor one. Against the identity-matched control, confirmation reduces label
switches from $23{,}303$ to $17{,}045$, a $26.9\%$ reduction at an essentially
equal instance budget ($10{,}413$ vs.\ $10{,}410$). It holds in $309$ of $312$ scenes (Wilcoxon
$p{=}1.5{\times}10^{-52}$, rank-biserial $0.99$; per-scene distribution in
Sec.~\ref{supp:indoor}). The three random-$K$ arms are
indistinguishable from the score-ranked control and far from the confirmation
module, so the reduction is specific to temporal selection rather than to
emitting fewer instances by any rule.

The secondary endpoint shows no material change: AP $0.1954$ against $0.1956$
unfiltered and $0.1926$ for the control. Unlike outdoors, the indoor gain
therefore does not come at a measurable detection-quality cost --- the two
pipelines differ in whether the withheld detections carry segmentation mass,
and we report the difference in effect without claiming the outdoor trade-off
is avoidable in general.

\noindent\textbf{The waiting is separable from the gain.} Provisional

emission with $11.29\%$ revisions and $104$ retractions.


\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Over an identical box set, association consistency improves and
detection-oriented accuracy degrades: the gain is bought rather than added, and
each further observation of delay buys more of it at proportionally more
detection cost. Against confidence accumulation it is a split rather than an
ordering --- persistence takes association consistency, accumulation keeps the
detection- and identity-oriented measures --- so the choice is which axis a
system is consumed along, not a ranking.

Confirmation must decide what to do with the observations that precede it, and
the classical rule offers two answers: release them late, or drop them and
lose the world-frame association gain with them. Neither suits a consumer that
must act immediately on output whose reliability improves later. The policy
separates \emph{when output becomes available} from \emph{when evidence
becomes reliable}, converting the cost from latency or retention into
revisability (Sec.~\ref{sec:consumer}): a record already held may be updated or withdrawn, and the
retraction horizon bounds how long that obligation lasts. It is an
operating-point change, not a new confirmation mechanism.

\subsection{Limitations}

\noindent\textbf{The revision endpoint is single.} AMOTA is the only
class-aware metric available to us; the class-agnostic family cannot respond
to a semantic-only change, and its invariance in Tab.~\ref{tab:policy} is
mechanical rather than a null result. Whether revision affects identity
consistency would need a class-aware association metric, which we do not have
and do not claim. Nor do we separate the label change from the nuScenes
attribute recomputation it entails, so the AMOTA gain is attributed to the
pair.

\noindent\textbf{The association gain does not transfer across detectors.}
Repeating the budget-matched comparison on BEVFusion-L --- LiDAR-only
BEVFusion with a TransFusion head --- reproduces the sensor-frame gain against
all three controls ($+0.0471$, $+0.0280$, $+0.0217$ AssA, every interval
excluding zero) but not the world-frame gain, which is negative against the
threshold control ($-0.0080$, $[{-}0.0144,{-}0.0015]$) and indistinguishable
from zero against both accumulation controls. The primary criterion of
Sec.~\ref{sec:stats} therefore fails on this detector, and we narrow the
association claim to the sensor frame; AMOTA stays positive in every cell, as
a point estimate. The tested arm is confirmation, so this bounds
Sec.~\ref{sec:detmatch} rather than the provisional policy;
Sec.~\ref{supp:crossdet} reports the analysis.

\noindent\textbf{Scope.} The consumer of Sec.~\ref{sec:consumer} is a proxy on
one detector: it shows whether an object is held when it comes close, not what
a planner does with it or whether the false-alert load is tolerable. ScanNet200 has no instance-tracking
ground truth, so the indoor endpoint is label switching rather than an identity
metric and tests whether the confirmation rule transfers, not whether the
streaming claim holds there.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Temporal confirmation provides a genuine association-consistency gain under
matched detection and identity budgets, but the gain is coupled to detection
retention and to latency, and on a second detector it survives only in the
sensor frame. Provisional emission separates immediate output from
evidence maturity: observations are emitted causally, then revised or retracted
as evidence accumulates. On nuScenes, $H{=}4$ retains $43.6\%$ more
observations than the prefix-deleting comparator and revision raises
AMOTA by $0.0823$ over the provisional output. Against the prefix-deleting arm
the policy gives up class-agnostic detection and identity quality. On
ScanNet200 the same rule reduces class-label switching by $26.9\%$ at a matched
identity count.
Confirmation is therefore best understood as an operating point in a
detection--association--latency--revisability trade-off rather than a
uniformly better tracker.
